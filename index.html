Immune profiling at scale

Johns Hopkins School of Medicine · Larman Lab · 2024–present

The PhIP-Seq platform

Develop and maintain the production PhIP-Seq pipeline and codespace on the JHU Rockfish HPC system, including phipmake2, an R library for post-alignment summarisation across multi-library screens.

When a run finishes, the reads and the metadata that says which library they belong to go through alignment, library splitting, beads-only background modelling, hit calling, annotation, and taxon-level aggregation. Which partition each step runs on is a judgment call rather than a fixed recipe.

Johns Hopkins School of Medicine · Larman Lab · 2024–present

PhIP-Seq antibody profiling across colorectal cancer cohorts

I lead the computational analysis for an R01-funded PhIP-Seq antibody-profiling project in Dr. Benjamin H. Larman's lab, across early-onset colorectal cancer, late-onset colorectal cancer, polyp, and healthy control cohorts, owning the analysis strategy, QC, statistics, and interpretation.

Differential reactivity is analysed at peptide, protein, pathway, and genus and species levels.

Developed CALIPER, a groupwise differential method for PhIP-Seq, and benchmarked it against existing tools using Boston Birth Cohort MMR vaccine data as ground truth. Contributed to developing and evaluating genus- and species-level aggregate reactivity scoring methods.

Functional antibody profiling

Johns Hopkins School of Medicine · Larman Lab · 2024–present

HAScan

A PhIP-Seq library of overlapping peptide tiles covering hemagglutinin across essentially all known influenza A and B strains. It asks where on HA a person’s antibodies bind, and which strains they recognise.

Every HA is aligned to a shared coordinate system before tiling, so the same position is comparable from one strain to the next and reactivity can be read as strain-specific or conserved. It pairs with InFlux: one assay says where antibodies bind, the other whether they block infection.

  • PhIP-Seq
  • Influenza
  • Serology

Molecular dynamics and free-energy landscapes

In the Kesavardhana Lab at the Indian Institute of Science, modelled hemagglutinin and neuraminidase variants embedded in a lipid bilayer and ran the simulations on them, looking at how hemagglutinin glycosylation patterns and neuraminidase stalk length affect sialic acid binding.
